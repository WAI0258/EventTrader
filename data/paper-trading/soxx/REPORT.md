# SOXX Investment Decision Report

**Review interval: June 4, 2026, 16:20 UTC to September 5, 2026, 00:00 UTC**

## Executive summary

The portfolio first traded a semiconductor demand reassessment, participated in the June recovery, and exited at the end of June. It stayed flat through July, then re-entered in August and briefly switched to a short position before rebuilding its long exposure.

The PM paper portfolio returned **+13.58%**, with a **6.30% maximum drawdown**. Direct, the counterfactual mapping of research assessments to positions, returned **−1.49%**, with a **12.10% maximum drawdown**. By the June 30 exit, the PM curve had already gained approximately **13.06%** relative to the interval's starting value. Most of the period's gains therefore accumulated in June; later decisions largely managed those gains rather than extending them substantially.

At the cutoff, the portfolio still held a **+0.5 target weight**. The latest assessment from a flat-position perspective was neutral: confirmatory news did not establish a fresh entry signal. For an existing long, however, memory demand and earnings dispersion still supported holding. The distinction was between initiating new exposure and managing exposure already held.

This is a retrospectively selected case: the September 5 cutoff was chosen after observing performance. The [full-interval results and selection explanation](../README.md) accompany it.

## Decision evolution

### June 4–8: trade the demand reassessment, then recognize recovery conditions

The recorded research treated Broadcom's AI revenue guidance shortfall as a sector demand-reassessment signal. Simultaneous weakness in individual semiconductor names and the ETF suggested that the shock extended beyond one company. The portfolio opened a **−0.5 short on June 4 at approximately 601.81**.

Subsequent assessments distinguished near-term AI capital-expenditure concerns from constraints in memory supply. HBM capacity allocation, reduced conventional memory supply, and a recovery from the lows weakened the case for continuing to short. On **June 8, the portfolio switched to a +0.5 long at approximately 553.24**.

The change did not erase the original negative event. It recognized that prices had repriced and that new evidence supported a recovery.

### June 8–17: fundamentals and price confirm recovery, while exposure remains adjustable

Equipment investment forecasts, memory demand, and AI infrastructure spending supported the recovery. SOXX reclaimed **618.84**, shifting the research framing from a rebound opportunity toward trend restoration.

On **June 15, the portfolio briefly increased its long target weight to 1.0, then reduced it to 0.5**. Direction and exposure strength were separate decisions: the breakout supported a long, while rapid appreciation and volatility limited the case for maintaining full exposure.

On June 16, recorded ASML guidance pressure and related equity weakness challenged the long thesis. At the same execution timestamp, the portfolio switched to a −0.5 short and then to flat. This did not create a sustained short-holding phase.

On **June 17, it rebuilt a +0.5 long at 611.24**, supported by equipment orders, memory-sector evidence, and the recovery structure. Later research upgrades did not automatically increase the executed position.

### June 30: exit as the remaining risk–reward compresses

Research had weakened by late June, but prices rebounded sharply. The portfolio did not interpret that rebound as an automatic reason to add exposure.

Near the **638.47** risk reference, the advance was mature and the remaining upside became less attractive relative to risk. The portfolio **closed at 636.66 on June 30**. The PM curve stood at approximately **+13.06%** at that timestamp.

The exit removed the June long exposure before the subsequent sector weakness. It was a portfolio action rather than merely a change in research wording.

### July–early August: continue research without mechanically trading each assessment

Equipment-stock weakness, leverage unwinding, uncertainty about AI investment returns, and macroeconomic pressure repeatedly supported cautious or bearish assessments. Memory supply and equipment investment nevertheless preserved a longer-term demand case.

The portfolio remained flat until August. A bearish research view did not require a new short, and a constructive view did not require immediate buying. Execution waited for actionable conditions rather than following every assessment change.

### August 11–20: re-enter, respond to a structural break, then reassess the short

On **August 11, the portfolio opened a +0.5 long at approximately 533.18**. Further recovery on August 13 supported continued participation without increasing exposure.

On August 18, a high-volume decline breached the **542.93** holding boundary. The portfolio treated this as a structural deterioration rather than routine volatility and **switched to a −0.5 short at approximately 531.35**.

By August 20, TSMC and memory-related evidence weakened the rationale for extending the short. The portfolio **closed the short at approximately 521.36**, then **rebuilt a +0.5 long at approximately 520.12**.

Long-term demand was not used to dismiss a price breakdown; the price decline was not used to dismiss evidence contradicting the short.

### Late August–September 4: retain measured exposure without treating confirmation as an add signal

AI investment returns, energy prices, and macroeconomic uncertainty continued to pressure the sector. Memory demand remained supportive, but the benefits were uneven across semiconductor companies.

The September 2 PM review maintained +0.5 exposure while monitoring whether weakness around **498.93** met the required persistent invalidation condition. By September 4, memory demand and selected earnings revisions supported the existing position. These were confirmations of an established thesis, not sufficient grounds for a fresh entry or increased exposure.

## Interpretation of the result

The gap between PM and Direct illustrates that research-update frequency and portfolio-trading frequency need not be identical. The June exit, July waiting period, and August responses to contradictory evidence affected the outcome.

It does not establish that every PM decision was correct. The August re-entry experienced drawdown, and the final long remained open. These selected-period results do not establish superiority across other periods or settings.

## Action framework at the cutoff

- **Existing long:** retain measured exposure; repeated confirmation of memory demand alone is not an add signal.
- **498.93:** monitor for persistent structural deterioration rather than defending the position solely on long-term AI demand.
- **489.215:** assess whether the recent recovery's starting structure remains intact.
- **532.9988:** look for renewed price acceptance above the upper reference.
- **New positions:** require fresh price and event confirmation; an existing position's holding rationale is not automatically an entry rationale.
