# Gold / IAU Investment Decision Report

**Review interval: July 20, 2026, 23:55 UTC to August 24, 2026, 23:55 UTC**

## Executive summary

The portfolio entered this interval with an existing +0.5 long, exited when near-term momentum deteriorated, and waited for additional macroeconomic confirmation before re-entering. Research turned constructive before the executed portfolio did.

The PM paper portfolio returned **+4.23%**, with a **1.84% maximum drawdown**. Direct, the counterfactual mapping of research assessments to positions, returned **+8.47%**, with a **1.50% maximum drawdown**. PM did not outperform Direct in this case: its later re-entry imposed a clear opportunity cost and did not produce a lower overall maximum drawdown within the selected interval.

At the cutoff, both the portfolio and the latest research remained moderately long. The advance had become more extended, however, and a potential dollar reversal and scheduled catalysts increased near-term risk. The framing favored holding and monitoring rather than chasing additional exposure.

This 35-day interval was selected retrospectively by maximizing observed PM return among daily endpoints at least 30 calendar days apart. The [full interval and selection explanation](../README.md) accompany it.

## Decision evolution

### July 20–23: preserve the longer-term demand view, but exit impaired near-term momentum

The opening position came from a **July 20 entry at 08:00 UTC, at 75.50**. Research considered gold demand, diplomatic developments, energy prices, and their transmission through the dollar and interest rates.

Geopolitical risk was not treated as unconditionally bullish for gold. Higher energy prices could strengthen inflation and policy concerns, increasing dollar and yield pressure enough to offset safe-haven demand.

On July 23, the PM review identified sustained price action below the **76.84** momentum boundary. The portfolio **closed at 76.01**. This addressed near-term momentum impairment without declaring that central-bank demand or gold's longer-term allocation rationale had disappeared.

### Late July: wait for policy and price pressure to ease

After the exit, research briefly became bearish. Dollar and policy pressure remained, and gold's behavior during market stress did not support unconditional safe-haven buying.

By July 30, the recorded policy outcome had not realized the more hawkish tail scenario, and price recovered from around **75.13**. Research shifted from bearish to neutral, but the portfolio did not immediately buy.

Weakening bearish conditions were not considered sufficient evidence that a new long was ready to execute.

### August 3–7: research restores a long view before the portfolio re-enters

On August 3, the analysis layer had restored a weak-long assessment. Support remained intact and prices were recovering, but dollar, yield, and volume conditions still qualified entry quality.

As the rebound accelerated, research remained constructive without upgrading the setup to a strong long. The portfolio stayed flat until recorded employment weakness and downward revisions provided additional support for dollar and yield relief.

On **August 7, the portfolio opened a +0.5 long at 81.77**.

At that timestamp, PM had gained approximately **0.47%** from the selected interval's starting value, while Direct had gained approximately **4.55%**. Earlier research exposure participated in the preceding advance; the executed portfolio paid the opportunity cost of waiting for confirmation.

### August 7–18: macroeconomic support broadens, but exposure does not increase

Employment, retail-demand, and other macroeconomic information progressively supported the dollar/yield-relief thesis. The case for holding gold no longer depended solely on geopolitical developments.

Energy prices and shipping risks could nevertheless reintroduce inflation pressure, while thin volume qualified parts of the advance. The portfolio therefore retained +0.5 exposure instead of escalating its position.

The long structure strengthened, but that did not remove the constraints on continuation quality.

### August 20–24: the advance continues, shifting attention toward nearer-term catalysts

IAU cleared the earlier **83.50** reference and extended its recovery from **81.36**. Recorded institutional-demand evidence added support for holding.

By August 24, the latest research reference was **87.595**. Research remained weak long but highlighted dollar-reversal risk, extended momentum, and a named catalyst that could compress the risk–reward of a fresh entry.

The older **76.84** boundary was now far below the current reference. It remained structurally relevant, but nearer price structure and dollar behavior had become more important for managing the existing position.

## Why hold without chasing

The holding case remained supported by an upward price structure, macroeconomic relief, and demand evidence. No subsequent exit had executed by the cutoff.

The entry case was less attractive after the advance. A new purchase would assume dollar-reversal and momentum-retracement risk at a higher price. A reasonable holding decision for an existing position was not automatically a reasonable fresh entry.

## Action framework at the cutoff

- **Existing long:** maintain measured exposure while prioritizing nearer-term risks; do not increase position strength merely because prices have risen.
- **81.36:** monitor whether the current recovery structure begins to deteriorate.
- **Dollar reversal:** reassess conviction if persistent dollar strength aligns with rising yields.
- **76.84:** retain it as a more distant momentum reference, rather than the only risk-management condition.
- **New positions:** wait for price acceptance after a pullback or fresh macroeconomic confirmation.
